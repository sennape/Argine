# Literature Positioning Refresh — Research 2 / Paper II

Retrieval date: **2026-11-16**. Web search via standard search; sources are arXiv preprints/aggregators current as of the retrieval date. Record here per-source retrieval note; prefer primary arXiv listings where resolvable.

## Landscape (MoE expert offloading / caching / prefetch / predictive routing / consumer-hardware inference)

1. **DAOP** (arXiv:2501.10375, DATE 2025) — data-aware GPU-CPU hybrid offloading with predictive pre-calculation; reports up to 8.20× over "traditional expert caching and prefetching" and 1.35× over offloading baselines.
2. **ProMoE** (arXiv:2410.22134) — proactive caching using a learned predictor over intermediate activations to predict *subsequent-layer* expert usage, combined with chunked prefetching, early preemption, and reordered inference to hide prefetch latency behind compute. Explicitly frames prior cache-based offloading as "reactive" (miss triggers load on the critical path) and positions proactive *prefetch* as the fix.
3. **FineMoE** (ACM, arXiv:2502.05370) — combines expert-selection-pattern prediction with semantic prompt hints to drive prefetching/caching/offloading jointly, targeting the latency-memory trade-off.
4. **ExpertFlow** (arXiv:2410.17954) — predictive expert caching plus token scheduling; explicitly contrasts itself with prior learned predictors (e.g., ProMoE) whose layer-by-layer sequential design limits scheduling flexibility, and with SE-MoE's non-predictive ring-buffer locality caching.
5. **PreScope** (arXiv:2509.23638) — prediction-driven expert *scheduling* (not just caching) with a learnable layer-aware predictor (LLaPoR), cross-layer scheduling that jointly optimizes prefetch cost and PCIe bandwidth contention, and an asynchronous I/O optimizer that **decouples I/O from computation** to eliminate "waiting bubbles" — reports 141% higher throughput, 74.6% lower latency vs. prior systems.
6. **Edge0** (arXiv:2609.18063, 2026) — closest in spirit to Argine's deployment target: consumer-hardware (single 24 GB machine), SSD-served, 35B-class MoE model. Uses a "prerouter" that predicts layer *N+1*'s routing one token ahead *during* layer *N*'s computation, so the predicted expert set is staged and the read is issued early enough to overlap with ongoing compute — directly addressing the critical-path timing problem ("layer N+1's experts must be chosen before layer N's output exists").
7. **Speculating Experts** (arXiv:2603.19289) — speculative-decoding-style expert pre-execution for MoE, evaluated explicitly on **Qwen3-30B-A3B**, the same model family as Argine's runtime validation; frames the CPU-GPU/host-store transfer latency as the dominant per-token-time bottleneck in memory-constrained MoE serving.
8. **MoE-SpeQ** (arXiv:2511.14102) — speculative decoding adapted to offloaded MoE, using a small draft model to predict which experts will be needed, again paired with active prefetch/scheduling rather than passive retention alone.
9. **Local Routing Consistency** (arXiv:2505.16056) — studies which MoE models are *amenable* to expert offloading at all, based on how consistent/local their routing is layer-to-layer — relevant background for why routing structure (Argine's R2-05/06/07/08) matters as a precondition for any of the above systems to work.

## Positioning Argine's contribution

Every system above that reports a measured throughput/latency win (ProMoE, FineMoE, ExpertFlow, PreScope, Edge0, Speculating Experts, MoE-SpeQ) pairs **prediction** with an **active mechanism that moves the fetch off the decode critical path** — proactive prefetching, cross-layer scheduling, compute/I/O overlap, or speculative pre-execution. None of them reports a throughput win from prediction feeding a purely *reactive* retention/eviction policy alone (i.e., a policy that only changes *which resident object is evicted when a miss already occurs*, without changing *when* the fetch is issued relative to compute).

Argine's R2-09→R2-12 programme tested exactly that narrower mechanism: a causal predictor (R2-10) driving a reactive retention/eviction policy (`PredictedProtectPolicy`, R2-BRIDGE-01/R2-11), with no prefetch, no scheduling, and no compute/I/O overlap component. The programme's R2-11 result (replay-level miss-byte-per-decode-step improvement) is consistent with the premise shared by all nine systems above — that predictive information about future expert use is real and exploitable — while R2-12's negative real-runtime result is consistent with the literature's own implicit thesis: **prediction alone, without an accompanying mechanism to act on that prediction before the miss reaches the critical path, is not expected to convert into measured decode-throughput improvement.** This literature survey is used in the manuscript to *sharpen* this scope distinction (reactive retention vs. proactive prefetch/scheduling/overlap) rather than to retrofit R2-12's negative result after the fact — the distinction between "changing what is evicted" and "changing when the fetch happens" was available and statable independent of R2-12's outcome.

## Explicit non-claims for the manuscript

- Argine does **not** claim that prediction alone should necessarily produce throughput gains (consistent with the task's explicit constraint and with the literature's consistent pairing of prediction with an active timing mechanism).
- Argine does **not** claim priority or superiority over any system above; none were re-implemented or benchmarked against Argine's own runtime in this program.
- The literature survey does not change, strengthen, or weaken any R2-09→R2-12 numerical result; it is used only in the manuscript's Related Work and Discussion sections to contextualize scope.
