Title: Diagnosing slow home Wi-Fi without replacing hardware

Most reported "slow Wi-Fi" problems are not caused by an underpowered
router but by one of a handful of fixable conditions, and working
through them in order resolves the large majority of cases before any
hardware purchase is needed.

First, check for channel congestion. Older 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi has only three
non-overlapping channels, and in dense housing many neighboring networks
default to the same one or two channels, causing interference that looks
like generic slowness rather than a dropped connection. Switching the
router to a less congested channel, or moving latency-sensitive devices
to the 5 GHz band where available, often fixes this immediately with no
other change. A free Wi-Fi analyzer app run from a phone can show which
channels are already crowded nearby.

Second, consider physical obstruction and distance. Wi-Fi signal strength
falls off faster through masonry, metal (such as a refrigerator or duct
work), and water (such as a fish tank or even a bathroom wall) than
through drywall or wood. A router placed in a closet or behind a TV
stand loses far more range than the same router placed centrally and
elevated. Before assuming the router itself is the bottleneck, it is
worth testing speed from a few different distances and rooms to see how
much of the problem is simply placement.

Third, check for a saturated internet connection rather than a wireless
problem at all. Running a wired speed test directly into the router,
compared against a wireless speed test from the same room, isolates
whether the bottleneck is the Wi-Fi link or the internet service itself.
If the wired speed is already far below the plan's advertised rate, no
Wi-Fi change will fix it, and the complaint is actually about the
internet service, not the local wireless network.

Finally, firmware age matters more than most people expect. Routers that
have not had a firmware update in several years can have inefficient or
buggy implementations of newer Wi-Fi standards, and a router reboot plus
a firmware check resolves a meaningful fraction of reported slowdowns
that have no other identifiable cause.
